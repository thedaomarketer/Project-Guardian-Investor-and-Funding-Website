# Investor Thesis

See `/funding` and `/business` for the public-facing version; canonical
data lives in `src/lib/content/funding.ts` (`investorThesisPoints`).

## The seven points

1. A large, emotionally important problem — caregiver awareness of a
   child's location and wellbeing is persistent and high-stakes.
2. Hardware plus recurring software revenue — a well-understood model in
   connected hardware with per-customer revenue that compounds.
3. Multiple form factors from one core — a modular SafeCore module designed
   to extend without re-engineering the platform each time.
4. An expandable software platform — the safety event engine, dashboard
   and emergency workflow are designed to generalize beyond the first
   hardware product.
5. Data-informed safety intelligence — location and device-status data as
   the foundation for explainable pattern detection as the platform
   matures (see `docs/ai-safety.md` for the hard limits on this).
6. A potential institutional market — schools, camps and youth
   organizations as a plausible future segment beyond individual families.
7. Founder-level understanding of the unknowns — the plan is built to
   validate hardware, regulatory and go-to-market assumptions in sequence,
   not skip them.

## The credibility argument

Per the master brief's core principle, carried through every page: "We
understand what we know. We understand what we don't know. We have a plan
to validate the unknowns." This is treated as a feature of the pitch, not
a hedge — a pre-commercial hardware company that pretends to have already
solved manufacturing, regulatory, and go-to-market risk is less credible
to a sophisticated investor than one that shows it understands the
sequence required to de-risk them.

## What this thesis explicitly does not do

Promise investment returns, imply guaranteed outcomes, or create urgency
("invest now before it's too late" language is explicitly banned in
`CLAUDE.md`). See `docs/risks.md` for the paired, equally honest risk
disclosure.
