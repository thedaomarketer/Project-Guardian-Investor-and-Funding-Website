## 1. Top-down market-size data found (publicly visible report landing pages)

These are the numbers visible on free report landing pages / press releases
of named research firms. **None of these were independently verified against
the underlying paid report** — they are the firm's own published headline
figures, captured via search-indexed landing-page copy. Report
methodologies, market definitions (e.g. what counts as "kids GPS tracker" vs
"wearable"), and regions covered differ substantially between firms, which is
why the numbers below disagree with each other by as much as 3–4x. This
disagreement is itself a useful, honest data point: it shows the segment is
too new/fragmented for a single authoritative TAM figure to exist yet.

## 2. Bottom-up TAM methodology (population inputs — for a future,
   clearly-labelled "illustrative estimate," not a finished number)

Per instructions, no final TAM was computed here. This section documents the
**inputs that are independently verifiable from primary government
statistics**, and the methodology a bottom-up estimate could follow once the
company supplies its own assumptions for the un-verified inputs (segment %
and spend/year), each of which must be labelled "Illustrative assumption."

### Methodology (to build later, in a data file, not here)

```
Bottom-up estimate = Total households with children
                      × % of those households that are "addressable"
                        (own smartphones, can afford ~$10-30/mo subscription,
                        have a child in the target age range, care about
                        location/safety tech — ALL ASSUMPTIONS)
                      × Estimated annual spend per adopting household
                        (device amortization + subscription — ASSUMPTION)
```

Only the first term below is a verified government statistic. The second and
third terms are business assumptions that Project Guardian's own team must
supply and label as "Illustrative assumption" — this research file does not
invent them.

### Verified population inputs

**Canada (Statistics Canada):**
- 2021 Census of Population: **10.3 million census families** in Canada, of
  which **6.0 million (58%)** included children. This is up from **5.6
  million families with children in 2011**, when they represented 60% of all
  census families (share slightly declined 2011→2021 even as the absolute
  count grew ~6%).
  Source: Statistics Canada, Census of Population 2021 (as reported via
  StatCan's "Families, households and marital status" statistics program).
- Statistics Canada also publishes annually updated **population estimates
  of census families and households** (non-census-year updates), with the
  most recent release covering **July 1, 2025** — "The Daily — Canada's
  population estimates: Census families and households, 2025" (released
  2025-11-07). This is the authoritative current-year source to pull an
  updated households-with-children count from (StatCan table 17-10-0061-01
  for general family counts; table 39-10-0041-01, released 2025-07-18, for
  census families with children by age of children). **The exact updated
  2025 households-with-children figure was not retrieved in this pass** (the
  StatCan data-table pages were not directly accessible in this research
  session) — flagged as a follow-up: pull the exact number from table
  39-10-0041-01 before publishing a Canada-specific bottom-up estimate.

**United States (US Census Bureau, Current Population Survey):**
- 2024 CPS data: **about 33.3 million (33,313 thousand) total US families
  with children under 18**, per the Census Bureau's "America's Families and
  Living Arrangements" release.
- About **39% of all US family households** included the householder's own
  children under 18 in 2024.
- Children were most commonly present in family households headed by
  40–44-year-olds (84% of those households included children).
  Source: US Census Bureau, "Four in Five Family Households With a
  Householder Ages 35–39 Included a Child Under 18 in 2024" (census.gov
  story, November 2024) and the associated HINC-04 / CPS tables.

### What is NOT yet verified (must stay "Illustrative assumption" in copy/data files)

- What % of households-with-children are realistic near-term buyers of a
  connected safety wearable + subscription (income threshold, child age
  range fit, existing smartphone/Life360-type app usage, willingness to pay
  for a *dedicated device* vs. a phone-based app).
- Average annual spend per adopting household (device price amortized +
  monthly subscription). Comparable pricing (Jiobit-style devices, Life360
  paid tiers, kids smartwatch retail prices) can inform a *range*, but
  picking a single number is a business assumption, not a researched fact.
- Canada vs. US vs. other-market split of any initial go-to-market.
- Any total dollar TAM/SAM/SOM figure — **not computed in this research
  pass, intentionally**, per instructions.

## 3. How to use this file in copy

- Any market-size sentence on the site must either (a) directly attribute a
  named vendor report ("According to Fortune Business Insights' Kids
  Smartwatch Market report, the global kids smartwatch market was valued at
  ~$2.65B in 2025..."), or (b) be built from the verified StatCan/Census
  population figures above combined with **explicitly labelled** assumptions
  ("Illustrative estimate, not independently verified").
- Do not average or reconcile the conflicting vendor numbers into a single
  "the market is worth $X" claim — that would fabricate false precision.
- The honest, defensible headline for an investor-facing market section is
  something like: *"Multiple industry research firms report the kids
  GPS-tracker and kids-smartwatch markets each in the low-to-high single
  digit billions of dollars globally as of 2024–2025, growing at
  double-digit CAGRs — see cited reports below. We have not independently
  verified these figures; reliable, granular public data for the specific
  connected-wearable-plus-subscription child-safety niche is limited, so our
  own sizing uses a bottom-up approach anchored to StatCan/Census household
  data (see methodology)."*
